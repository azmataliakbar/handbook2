## What This Is

This is a **Knowledge System of Record (KSoR)** — a single source of truth for organizational knowledge that is published to multiple surfaces simultaneously. Whether you read it as a person on this website or an AI agent queries it through the MCP, you receive the same governed, approved knowledge.

## Why It Matters

Traditional systems of record (ledgers, databases, HR systems) establish what is _true about current state_. A Knowledge System of Record establishes what the organization _knows and how it should operate_.

This handbook is authoritative for:
- What a Knowledge System of Record is
- How a project climbs the governance ladder
- Which surfaces publish the same governed knowledge

## One Source, Many Surfaces

A person reads pages. An agent reads bytes. Both are served from the same governed markdown, which is what makes them answer alike.

The knowledge you see here:
- **On this website** — published as readable pages with governance displayed
- **Through the MCP agent surface** — served as searchable, cited retrieval with full approval chains
- **In llms.txt** — available as plain text for AI systems

Edit once, publish everywhere.

## How Knowledge Gets Here

Every document in this handbook:
- Starts as authored markdown in the `knowledge/` folder
- Is checked for format and consistency
- Is approved by an authorized person or process
- Is embedded into a vector database for semantic search
- Is published to all surfaces at once

When you query this handbook through the agent interface (the MCP), you get back not just the answer, but its provenance: who approved it, when, and under what authority.

## The Governance Ladder

Knowledge here climbs a ladder of rigor:

1. **Draft** — written, not yet approved
2. **Stable** — approved and published to all surfaces
3. **Deprecated** — replaced, but kept for history with a pointer to its successor

You can see the approval chain on every published document. No claim here travels without its evidence.
